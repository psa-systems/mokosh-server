# Billing invariants

Scope: Tax, invoices, balance, overdue, write-offs, payment terms, products, prepaid blocks, currency and the financial read gates.

Each section below is a convention moved verbatim from the repo `CLAUDE.md` (PMS-1437), which keeps a one-line index entry linking here. A new convention adds its full text here and one index line in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md).

## Billable time (PMS-944)

Billable time is armed at creation, not at approval (PMS-944): a `time_entries` row gets `billing_status = 'ready_to_bill'` from `resolve_billing_status` when it is created, and `create_invoice_from_time_entries` bills only that status, so a billable client hour is invoiceable because it was logged. PMS-144 had `approve_timesheet` write that status and it was the ONLY statement that ever did, which made countersigning a week the sole route to an invoice and left a tenant with timesheets off (PMS-943) unable to bill at all. `MileageTrackingService::create` never had the gate and is the shape both now share. Three consequences worth knowing before touching this area. An update must re-resolve the status, or an entry marked non-billable keeps the arming it was created with and is invoiced anyway; passing the current status in is what stops an already-`billed` row being re-armed onto a second invoice. Project and task `actual_hours` / `actual_amount` count `approval_status <> 'rejected'` rather than `= 'approved'`, because nothing on a timesheets-off tenant ever reaches `approved` and every project there would read zero. And approval of a unit of work now lives only where timesheets do: the `/time-entries/{id}/approvals` routes carry `RequireTimesheets`, while `change_request` and `quote` approvals are untouched because they approve a decision rather than a unit of work, and `ticket` approvals never gated anything (`tickets` has no approval column and `ApprovalsService::decide` writes only its own table). Block-hours consumption moved off approval entirely in PMS-951 (see the storage-adjacent bullet below), for the same two reasons: nothing on a timesheets-off tenant reaches approval, and an entry is real when it is logged.

## Quote tax (PMS-1038)

A quote's tax follows the same rule (PMS-1038), with one difference in shape: quote lines are mutated one request at a time, so `QuotesService::recompute_totals`, which already runs after every line change, is the rule (a quote with a `tax_rate` re-derives, one with none keeps the amount it was given) and `settle_tax_rate` decides the state: a given amount clears the rate, a named rate sets it, and only a create falls back to the tenant's default. The rate is frozen on the quote because the customer signs the total. The two rate lookups are `BillingService::assert_tax_rate_in_tenant` and `default_tax_rate`, shared rather than copied.

## Invoice tax (PMS-1029)

Tax is derived from the tenant's rate over the taxable lines (PMS-1029): `BillingService::apply_tax` is the one rule, run after an invoice's lines are written and in the same transaction, by every writer (`create_invoice`, `create_invoice_from_time_entries`, the recurring generator, and `update_invoice` when the lines are replaced or a rate or amount is named). The rate is the request's `tax_rate_id` (validated in the tenant and active, the PMS-333 reason), else the tenant's active default; `tax_amount = round(taxable line total * rate / 100, 2)` half away from zero; the rate is FROZEN on the invoice (`tax_rate_id`, `tax_rate`) so the document prints it and a later edit to `tax_rates` does not re-price an issued document, the PMS-990 `net_days` shape. A given `tax_amount` is stored as given and records no rate, because a jurisdiction the rule cannot express exists and the SPA sends one today. `invoice_lines.is_taxable` is stored per line, and a product line copies `products.is_taxable` in the INSERT itself, never read through, for the PMS-955 reason. The rate is a PERCENT everywhere (the validator accepts 0 to 100, the SPA divides by 100), which is why migration 189 widened `tax_rates.rate` from `DECIMAL(5,4)`, a column that could not hold 10. `apply_tax` writes `total` and `balance_due` the way `update_invoice` does and deliberately NOT through `recompute_invoice_balance`: that derives `status` for a payment event and its fallback arm is `sent`, which would flip a draft.

## Balance (PMS-953)

An invoice's balance is derived in one place (PMS-953): `BillingService::recompute_invoice_balance` owns `amount_paid`, `amount_credited`, `balance_due`, `status` and `paid_at`, computing all five from SQL sums over `payments`, `payment_refunds` and issued `credit_notes` rather than from Rust-side arithmetic. Every write that moves money calls it while holding the invoice row lock from `lock_invoice_totals`, in that order, so a credit racing a payment queues instead of losing one of the two writes (PMS-695). Adding a second updater for any of those five columns is the mistake this guards against: two writers with two rules for one balance is how the halves come to disagree. An issued invoice is immutable and the only correction is a credit note, which is what finally gave `void` a writer; before PMS-953 that status and `written_off` were values the model knew and no code path could reach, because PMS-38 and PMS-39 both deferred the correcting half and only the freezing half shipped. A credit note is issued or voided and never edited, for the reason its invoice is not: the customer holds a copy of both. A statement (PMS-954, `GET /statements`) is a read model over those same rows and writes nothing: it reads only DATED columns (`invoice_date`, `payment_date`, `issue_date`, a refund's `created_at`), never `balance_due` or `amount_paid`, because those are current values and a statement for a period that closed last month has to show what was outstanding then. Its opening balance is computed from everything before the period rather than stored as a running total, for the same reason the balance has one writer: a running total is a third home for a number that already has one, and the only one that can be silently wrong.

## Amendment (PMS-1334)

A sent invoice is replaced, not edited, and the replacement is what voids it (PMS-1334): `POST /invoices/{id}/amend` answers a new DRAFT carrying the original's lines and naming it in `invoices.amends_invoice_id`, and the invoice it amends is untouched until that draft is SENT, at which point the sending transaction voids it with `void_reason` naming the replacement. The model is Stripe's revision (docs.stripe.com/invoicing/invoice-edits) rather than a local invention, because "how is a finalized invoice corrected" is a question with an industry answer and PMS-953 had only half of it: a credit note is right for an invoice somebody has paid and wrong for one nobody has, where it means three documents for one mistake. The ordering is the design: nothing is corrected until the replacement goes out, so an operator can start an amendment and delete it. Every refusal names the instrument that fits instead (a draft is edited, a paid or credited invoice is credited, a void has already been replaced, a second draft amendment waits for the first), and the preconditions are checked twice, at create and again in the send transaction, because a payment can land against the original while the draft sits. The link is stored one way: the amendment names what it replaces, and `amended_by` is resolved by looking for the invoice that names it, on the detail read only, because Stripe's stored reverse pointer (`latest_revision`) is a second home for a derived fact. An amendment inherits no `sent_at`, `emailed_at`, `emailed_to` or `issuer_snapshot`, which is why the copy is column by column rather than wholesale, and it is dated today with its due date re-derived so it cannot arrive overdue. One consequence worth knowing: `STATEMENT_ISSUED_INVOICE` still excludes `void`, so an amended-away invoice leaves the statement, which was previously justified by "it never reached a customer" and now rests on a void being zero-value while the amendment beside it carries the whole corrected total.

## Overdue (PMS-1037)

Overdue is derived, and reminders are a worker (PMS-1037): `billing::models::overdue_days` is the one rule (`sent` or `partially_paid`, a balance, `due_date` before the tenant's today), applied by `InvoiceResponse::mark_overdue` on the list and the detail and as SQL by `?overdue=true`; nothing stores it, for the PMS-953 reason `balance_due` is derived. `BillingService::send_due_reminders` runs hourly through `InvoiceReminderWorker` (built with delivery in `main`, because it mails): `billing_reminders/{enabled,schedule,send_hour}` from `read_invoice_reminder_settings`, the tenant's local hour and day from `read_tenant_zone`, the recipient from `emailed_to` else the billing contact, the stored document attached, and `invoice_reminders` claimed with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` before the send as the once-per-step guard. No late fees: a fee is a new line on a new document.

## Write-off (PMS-1036)

A write-off is not a credit (PMS-1036): `BillingService::write_off_invoice` (`POST /invoices/{id}/write-off`, finance only, reason required) moves a `sent` or `partially_paid` invoice to `written_off`, the status the model had known since migration 010 with nothing writing it, and records who, when, why and `write_off_amount`, the balance at that moment frozen. A credit note says the customer did not owe this and reduces revenue; a write-off says they owe it and will not pay, a bad-debt expense, so `balance_due` is left as it was and a later payment is a recovery, kept with the status standing: `recompute_invoice_balance`'s status CASE reads `written_off_at` first, because that CASE runs on every payment or credit event and would otherwise flip the invoice back to `partially_paid`. The statement carries a write-off as its own line kind (`write_offs`, `total_written_off`), dated by the write-off, and takes it out of the closing balance. No reversal in v1.

## Sending (PMS-991, PMS-992)

Sending an invoice emails the invoice, and `sent` means it went (PMS-991, PMS-992): on the first transition to `sent`, `update_invoice` resolves the recipient BEFORE the transition (the invoice's billing contact, else the company's `default_billing_contact_id`, either with an address; no guessing beyond that, because PMS-993 settles the recipient as a contact holding the billing role and rejects picking "some contact"), refuses the send with a 409 naming the company and what is missing when none resolves, and otherwise freezes the invoice, stores the document (PMS-959), and emails it INSIDE the transaction through `email_invoice`, with the document attached (`Mailer::send_with_attachments`) and the portal pay link added only when a gateway is connected. A relay that refuses the message rolls the transition back, so the invoice stays a draft rather than a `sent` nobody received; a send that goes records `emailed_to` and `emailed_at`. `skip_email: true` on the update is the explicit path for an invoice delivered by hand: it freezes and records nobody as emailed, which is what the seeders and the suites that send without a contact say. Because storage is not transactional, a rolled-back send can leave a stale render at the document's key, which is why `GET /invoices/{id}/pdf` reads the store only for a frozen invoice and renders a draft live. Before PMS-991 the mail was gated on the gateway and a fresh install mailed nobody; the "no email rule matches this action" the standup reported was the client's preview modal (MAPPS-642). `SharedMailer` on the test app (`app.mailer.swap`) is how a suite asserts on what the API sent, or refuses it.

## Net terms (PMS-990)

Net terms are a number on the term (PMS-990): `payment_terms.net_days` is what "Net 30" means, and `BillingService::resolve_due_date` is the one rule for a due date the caller did not give: the invoice date plus the net days of the named term, or of the tenant's active default term when none is named, with the term that supplied the count recorded on the invoice; a term with no count (an "On approval" is legitimate) or a tenant with no default falls back to thirty days, the value the time-entry and recurring paths hardcoded before, so nothing moves for a tenant that configured nothing. A given due date is stored as given. Changing an invoice's term without a due date re-derives it, because a Net 15 invoice keeping a Net 30 date carries a term its own dates contradict; changing nothing leaves it alone. The invoice document prints no status line: the bytes are stored at the first send and served unchanged, so a status would read `sent` forever, and a draft preview that printed `draft` would differ from the stored document by that one word. `a_draft_preview_is_byte_identical_to_the_document_stored_at_send` in `tests/issued_documents.rs` pins that preview and send agree, which is what "preview before send" is here: a live render of a draft (`GET /invoices/{id}/pdf`) plus the store at send, not a stored draft render.

## Products (PMS-955)

Product catalog and pricing (PMS-955): `products` is a tenant-scoped price list, and `invoice_lines.product_id` / `contract_items.product_id` are optional references to it. The price is NEVER read through the reference. A line stores `unit_price` at the moment it is written and the recurring worker copies `contract_items.unit_price` onto the line it generates, so editing the catalog cannot re-price a signed contract or a document already issued, which matters because an issued invoice is immutable (PMS-953). A product reference is validated against the caller's tenant on write (`assert_product_sellable`), for the same reason `payment_term_id` is (PMS-333): an FK check bypasses RLS, so a foreign-tenant id would satisfy the constraint and link silently. A sold product cannot be deleted (a plain FK reference refuses it, surfaced as a 409) and is retired with `is_active = false` instead; `ProductResponse.in_use` (PMS-1002) says up front whether anything names it, computed as an `EXISTS` on each FK column in the one `PRODUCT_COLUMNS` list every product read and `RETURNING` shares, so a client can withhold Delete where it would be refused. It is advisory and the FK stays the guard. Products price things; `rate_cards` prices labour by work type and by the hour: two axes, not two homes for one thing. Whether a contract item bills is `contract_items.billing_rule` (PMS-956), not `item_type`: `every_period`, `once` or `manual`, because `item_type` says what the item IS and the two were one field, which is how a `product` item came to bill never. The generator selects on the rule, and a `once` item is claimed with `UPDATE ... WHERE billed_at IS NULL` inside the generating transaction, because `contract_invoice_runs` is keyed on `(tenant_id, contract_id, period_start)` and so records that a PERIOD was billed: without the per-item claim a setup fee added in March bills again in April. Omitting the rule on write derives it from `item_type` (`BillingRule::derive`), reproducing today's behaviour for the two types that already billed. The column default is `manual` and the migration backfilled only `recurring_service` and `retainer`, both for one reason: a row nobody was charging for must not start charging, since a missing charge is recoverable where a wrong one is a conversation. `contract_items.billing_frequency` is still read by nothing and was deliberately not overloaded, because its name promises per-item cadence that nothing implements.

## Prepaid draw (PMS-951)

A prepaid block is drawn down when time is logged (PMS-951): `time_entries.contract_id` is derived from the company at create and stop-timer, and `consume_for_entry` draws the hours immediately. It was never set by anything before, so `consume_approved_hours` had read a column no write path wrote since PMS-405 and a block-hours contract was never consumed at all: an MSP billed hourly for work a client had prepaid. Approval is NOT a consumption point and must not become one again, because PMS-943 gates it behind the timesheets module (a tenant with it off would draw nothing) and PMS-944 already made an entry real at creation (so both would draw). `time_entries.hours_consumed` is the APPLIED hours, never the duration, because ten hours against an eight-hour allotment applies eight and leaves two as overage and only the applied part left the balance; `hours_balance_id` names the period row it left, so an entry whose date moves gives back to the period that gave. Both together are the idempotency guard: every claim is `WHERE hours_consumed IS NULL`. An edit releases and re-draws rather than adjusting by a delta, which covers a duration change, a flip to non-billable and a date crossing a period boundary without a case for each. Only billable `client` work draws. A release reads the old values from a `FOR UPDATE` sub-select and not from `RETURNING`, which in Postgres yields the row after the update and would hand back the NULL it just wrote.

## Overage (PMS-1035)

Prepaid block hours are not invoiced again, and overage bills at the overage rate (PMS-1035): the PMS-951 draw is also the billing signal. `consume_for_entry` stamps an entry fully inside the allotment `billing_status = 'prepaid'`, a fourth value beside `not_billed`, `ready_to_bill` and `billed` (migration 191), and `create_invoice_from_time_entries` selects `ready_to_bill` only, so the hours the customer already paid for never reach an hourly invoice; the refusal message names them as covered rather than as not ready. An entry that ran past the block keeps `ready_to_bill` and records `overage_hours` and `overage_rate` (the contract item's rate at the draw, NULL when it names none) beside `hours_consumed`, and the builder bills such an entry for the overage hours alone, at that rate else the entry's own, with `descriptions::time_entry_overage_line` saying so. `prepaid` is not sticky the way `billed` is: `release_for_entry` clears the overage columns and puts a prepaid entry back to `ready_to_bill`, and an edit's re-draw decides afresh. Not `billed`, because that means "on an invoice"; the SPA's `BillingStatus` comes from `mokosh-types`, so the label for the new value is a pin bump on the client (the MAPPS follow-up).

## Currency (PMS-1028)

An invoice is issued in the tenant's currency (PMS-1028): `settings::read_default_currency` reads `billing_prefs/currency` on the caller's tenant-GUC connection, unset means `USD`, and every invoice writer (`create_invoice`, `create_invoice_from_time_entries`, the recurring generator) resolves the request's currency else the setting; a credit note inherits its invoice's. `invoices.currency` is the per-row home, so an invoice keeps the currency it was issued in when the setting changes. The two `"USD"` literals left in `billing::service` are read-side fallbacks for rows older than the column and say so.

## Read gates (PMS-962)

A financial read carries two gates (PMS-962): every handler in `src/modules/billing/routes.rs` takes `RequireBilling` (the per-tenant module gate, 404 when billing is off) AND `_finance: RequireFinance` (the role gate, `super_admin | admin | finance`). Six read routes carried only the first, so a technician refused `GET /invoices` was served `GET /statements`, which holds the same invoice totals plus the whole payment history: the shape of defect PMS-350 exists to have closed. It was not one slip but the same one three times (PMS-953's credit-note reads, PMS-954's statement, PMS-955's product reads), so the rule is enforced in source rather than remembered: `billing::routes::finance_gate::every_billing_handler_also_takes_the_finance_gate` reads the file's own text under `cargo test --lib` and fails on a handler taking one gate without the other, with an `UNGATED` exception list that is empty and has its own test saying so. Adding a read route to that file means adding both extractors, or naming it in `UNGATED` with the reason every role may read it. `rbac_route_coverage.rs` is the behavioural half: the source guard proves the extractor is present, the matrix proves it does what it is there for.

## Dual-plane gates (PMS-1041)

The five dual-plane invoice handlers carry the same two gates in the body (PMS-1041): `get_invoice`, `list_invoices`, `get_invoice_pdf`, `pay_invoice` and `get_invoice_payment_readiness` serve a contact session as well as a staff one, so they take `RequireCallerContext` (stacking `RequireBilling` on top would resolve the caller twice, giving two answers to "who is this" on one request) and their staff branch calls `assert_staff_billing_finance` instead. That helper reproduced only the ROLE half, so a tenant that had switched billing off still listed invoices, read one, downloaded its PDF, checked pay readiness and started a payment: the whole invoice surface, which is the part of billing a customer is most likely to have turned off. It now runs the module check FIRST and the role check second, the order stacking `RequireBilling` ahead of `RequireFinance` produced, so a disabled module is 404 for every staff role rather than 403 for some. `every_billing_handler_also_takes_the_finance_gate` could not see this: it filters on `RequireBilling` before it asserts anything, so a handler carrying NEITHER gate was skipped rather than flagged and its `seen > 20` tripwire stayed healthy on the other thirty. `every_billing_handler_carries_the_module_gate` beside it asserts the complement - every `async fn` in the file takes `RequireBilling` or calls the helper, and the second set is exactly the five named in `INLINE_STAFF_GATE`, so a sixth is a deliberate act. Whether a CONTACT session should also see 404 when the MSP disables billing is a separate product question and is deliberately not decided here: the contact branch gates on the PMS-1025 `caps::INVOICES_*` capabilities and is untouched.
